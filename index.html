About me

I moved to the United States when I was seven and began school in first grade. Over the years, computers moved from being a hobby to a clear passion — I started with Scratch and Code.org lessons, then built simple school websites. Each project pushed me to learn a new concept: HTML and CSS for layouts, Python for logic, then data analysis and machine learning for research questions, and fundamentals of cybersecurity for safe systems.

My COVID-19 research used public datasets from Johns Hopkins to explore trends in infections and outcomes across countries; I documented my process in reproducible notebooks and presented key findings at a symposium in College Park, MD. That project taught me not only technical skills — data cleaning, visualization, and basic statistical testing — but also how to explain technical results to an audience from many backgrounds.

To turn learning into service, I launched a Python Bootcamp and teach students overseas who are beginning their programming journeys. Teaching has sharpened my explanations, improved my patience, and shown me how empowered learners can become when given the right guidance. I also launched NAFA Samaj, a community app that reflects my interest in building tools that help people connect and share resources.

Outside of technology, I play club soccer for EagleFC. Competing on the field taught me how to manage time, lead small teams, and keep pushing when a solution isn’t obvious — the same persistence that helps me debug tricky code. I’m applying to study Computer Science in college because I want to advance my technical skills and scale the impact of the projects I build — especially those that help communities and create learning opportunities for others.

If you’d like, I can share more detailed project pages (with screenshots, code, and slides), a one-page résumé, or a downloadable portfolio PDF. I’m also happy to add short video demos or a few quick testimonials from students I’ve taught.
