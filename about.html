About Me

My interest in computer science didn’t begin with code — it began with curiosity. When I was younger, I wondered how technology could connect people who were far apart, or how something as simple as a message or shared space online could make someone feel understood. That question stayed with me as I grew older and moved to a new country, new schools, and new communities.

Over time, that curiosity led me into programming, data science, and app development. I learned how patterns in data can tell stories about people, health, and society. I built a community-focused app for the Nepalese Association in Arizona to help families stay connected. And I started a Python bootcamp for students in Nepal — something I wish I had access to when I was first discovering technology.

I’m especially interested in data science and AI as tools to better understand the world: not for the sake of prediction alone, but to support thoughtful decision-making and meaningful impact. To me, computer science is a way to help people feel supported, informed, and connected. I hope to continue learning in environments where curiosity, collaboration, and compassion guide the work.
